# Wordmark Logo Design (type-led, idea-first)

This skill applies a type-led identity approach associated with studios such as Pentagram: the logo carries one clear idea, usually expressed through the letters of the name itself. It is a working method built from general design principles, not a reproduction of any studio's work. Never copy, trace or closely imitate an existing logo or trademark; every concept must be original.

## Principles

1. **One idea.** Every mark expresses a single thought that can be explained in one sentence. If it needs two sentences, cut something.
2. **Wordmark first.** Start from the name's letterforms. Add a separate symbol only if it earns its place (works alone as an app icon / favicon and says something the wordmark can't).
3. **Find the idea inside the letters.** Look for shapes, overlaps, ratios, negative space or a letter that can carry the meaning (e.g. an "x" as a multiplier or a meeting point, a numeral as a quantity).
4. **Restraint.** One typeface family, one or two weights, at most one accent colour. No gradients, shadows, bevels or trend effects.
5. **Modified, not default.** Start from a solid typeface (geometric or grotesque sans), then make one or two deliberate custom changes that carry the idea. Unmodified fonts aren't a logo.
6. **Works in one colour.** Black on white and white on black must both work before any colour is added.
7. **Scales.** Legible at 16 px (favicon) and at billboard size. Check counters, spacing and thin strokes at small sizes.
8. **System, not just a mark.** Show how the idea extends: a short form / monogram, a favicon, and one application (e.g. a business card or app tile).
9. **Ages well.** Prefer timeless geometry over fashionable styling.

## Workflow

1. **Brief.** Capture: the name, what the business does, audience, the one idea the name should carry, words it should feel like (and must not feel like), and constraints (colours, existing assets, competitors to avoid looking like).
2. **Idea list.** Write 5–8 one-sentence ideas the mark could express, drawn from the name and the brief. Pick the strongest 3–4.
3. **Concepts.** For each idea, build an original SVG concept:
   - Use `<text>` with a widely available web font (e.g. Inter, Space Grotesk, Manrope via Google Fonts) or construct letters from geometric paths.
   - Keep the canvas simple (e.g. viewBox 0 0 600 200), flat fills, `currentColor` where possible so it can flip light/dark.
   - Provide the full wordmark plus a compact monogram/favicon version.
4. **Rationale.** For each concept: the one-sentence idea, what was modified and why, and where it might struggle.
5. **Tests.** Check every concept against: one-colour, 16 px, reversed (white on dark), and "can someone draw it from memory after one look?" Drop concepts that fail.
6. **Recommend.** Pick one, explain why, and list next refinement steps (custom letter drawing, spacing, colour, trademark check).

## Presenting

Show concepts side by side on a neutral background, each in black-on-white and white-on-black, with the favicon at actual small size next to it. Keep rationale short and plain. Remind the user that final logos need a trademark search before use and benefit from refinement by a professional type designer.
